Think of each program 
as a musical symphony. 
Each line as a note. 
Each method as a chord. 
Making harmony together. 

---

Thinking of **each programme as a symphony** is a metaphor for viewing software as a system of collaborating parts. Classes, modules, data and interfaces may be simple individually, but their value becomes apparent when they work together coherently.

Good design does not aim for each component to be sophisticated, but rather for it to have a clear purpose and fit in with the others. This ties in with [[julzdao/computer-science/Separation of Concerns SoC]], cohesion and [[julzdao/computer-science/Coupling]].

The "Passes the tests" from the primordial rule from [Kent Beck](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/BeckDesignRules.html) would be like making sure that all of the instruments playing in the symphony are in tune. If you happen to play the guitar, you have probably experienced what is playing the guitar with one or two strings out of tune. It is not pleasant. Same with programming. Make sure to pass all the tests, so the symphony is in tune. 

> [!tip]
> When part of a programme seems too complex, don’t just look at that one part. Also ask yourself **what role it is trying to play within the whole**.

The metaphor also serves as a reminder that programming isn’t just about making the code work: there is a dimension of structure, rhythm and composition that ties in with [[julzdao/computer-science/Programming as an Expression]].
